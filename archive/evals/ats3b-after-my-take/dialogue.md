Junior developers who rely on AI assistants are learning less

I've managed engineering teams for twelve years, and something has changed in the last two. The junior developers who joined us recently ship code faster than any cohort I've hired, but when something breaks, many of them are stuck. They paste the error into an assistant and try whatever comes back.

A survey I ran last month of 200 developers in my network backs this up: 61% of those with under two years' experience said they "often" accept AI suggestions they don't fully understand, against 22% of seniors.

We've seen this before with calculators. Students who leaned on them early never built number sense, and many of them struggle with estimation as adults.

The fix isn't to ban the tools. It's to make juniors earn them: no assistant for their first three months, and code reviews that ask "why does this work?" rather than "does this work?". Teams that skip this will end up with developers who can produce code but can't maintain it.
